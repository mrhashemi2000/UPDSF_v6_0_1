## 🔬 Figure 4: UV Robustness & Selection-Destruction Trade-Off Analysis

### 1. Technical Overview ("The What")
This 4-panel robustness analysis tests the model's sensitivity to extreme variations in UV photon flux ($10^8$ to $10^{18}$ photons/cm²/s) across multiple quantum yield scales ($q_y = 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$). It tracks the Chemical Selection Index (CSI), direct environmental discrimination, cumulative UV damage events, and final total DNA yield.

### 2. Scientific Insight ("The So What")
- *Radiation Threshold Behavior: All metric curves remain completely flat and stable up to a critical threshold (approx. $10^{14}$ to $10^{15}$ photons/cm²/s), proving that the system's core selection dynamics are robust against low-to-moderate radiation fluctuations.
- The UV Selection-Destruction Trade-Off: While extreme UV flux sharply accelerates the Chemical Selection Index (panels 1–3), it simultaneously triggers a catastrophic collapse in total DNA yield (panel 4). This inverse relationship demonstrates a fundamental physical trade-off between selection efficiency and molecular survival.
- Parameter Independence ($q_y$ Scaling): Varying the quantum yield across two orders of magnitude systematically shifts the curves without altering qualitative behavior, confirming that the model's conclusions are independent of absolute UV parameter tuning.

### 3. Key Metric Highlights
- Stability Zone: Zero measurable UV damage or selection drift occurs below $10^{13}$ photons/cm²/s.
- CSI Surge: Selection index jumps from baseline ($\sim 2.6$) to over $4.5$ at extreme UV fluxes ($10^{18}$).
- Yield Collapse: Total DNA survival plummets to near-zero for high quantum yields ($q_y = 10^{-2}$) under unmitigated maximum flux.

### 4. Prebiotic Implication
This figure provides crucial computational evidence for the setting of early prebiotic evolution. Because unshielded high-UV fluxes destroy total DNA yield despite boosting selection, primitive genetic polymers must have emerged within protected, shielded microenvironments—such as submarine hydrothermal vent pores or deep mineral sediments—that successfully balanced chemical filtering with molecular preservation.
